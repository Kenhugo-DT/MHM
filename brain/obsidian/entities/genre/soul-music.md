# Soul music

## Summary

Vocal music that became a foundation for Motown-era pop and later R&B.

## Connections

- [[aretha-franklin|Aretha Franklin]] - soul singer (Franklin became a defining voice of soul, not merely a generic pop performer.)
- [[gospel-music|Gospel music]] - gospel to soul (Gospel vocal practices helped shape secular soul singing.)
- [[phil-collins|Phil Collins]] - soul influence (Collins brought soul material into his 1980s work.)
- [[pop-music|Pop music]] - Motown pop-soul bridge (The Supremes bridged pop and soul audiences.)
- [[ray-charles|Ray Charles]] - soul pioneer (Charles's fusion of gospel expression and R&B helped define soul.)
- [[rhythm-and-blues|Rhythm and blues]] - R&B to soul (Soul grew from R&B while developing a more pronounced gospel-derived vocal language.)
- [[stevie-wonder|Stevie Wonder]] - Motown soul (Stevie Wonder's career developed within Motown's soul tradition.)
- [[the-jackson-5|The Jackson 5]] - Motown vocal group (The Jackson 5 blended vocal-group, soul and Motown influences.)
- [[the-supremes|The Supremes]] - Motown soul (The Supremes' vocal sound bridged soul and pop.)
