# Gospel music

## Summary

African American sacred music whose vocal and rhythmic practices shaped R&B, soul and early rock and roll.

## Connections

- [[aretha-franklin|Aretha Franklin]] - gospel roots (Franklin sang in church before her secular career; gospel remained a vocal foundation.)
- [[ray-charles|Ray Charles]] - gospel vocabulary (Gospel was a source for Charles's secular music; this edge describes influence, not a gospel-only career.)
- [[rhythm-and-blues|Rhythm and blues]] - gospel roots (Gospel was one of several sources of R&B; the two traditions should remain distinct.)
- [[rock-and-roll|Rock and roll]] - gospel to rock and roll (The gospel tradition was one of rock and roll's documented early influences.)
- [[sister-rosetta-tharpe|Sister Rosetta Tharpe]] - electric gospel (Tharpe brought a powerful electric-guitar sound to gospel performance.)
- [[soul-music|Soul music]] - gospel to soul (Gospel vocal practices helped shape secular soul singing.)

## Sources

- [Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://rockhall.com/exhibitions/roots-of-rock/)

## Curator Notes

Keep gospel a distinct sacred tradition. Its documented influence on R&B and soul is a historical bridge, not evidence that the genres are interchangeable.
