# Rhythm and blues

## Summary

A broad African American popular-music tradition drawing on blues, gospel and swing, and feeding soul and rock and roll.

## Connections

- [[aretha-franklin|Aretha Franklin]] - R&B voice (Franklin's recordings brought gospel-inflected singing into R&B.)
- [[blues|Blues]] - blues roots (Early R&B drew on blues song forms and performance practices; this is a lineage, not a synonym.)
- [[buck-owens|Buck Owens]] - R&B repertoire (Owens played R&B material in Bakersfield dance halls; this is a repertoire bridge, not a claim that he was an R&B artist.)
- [[gospel-music|Gospel music]] - gospel roots (Gospel was one of several sources of R&B; the two traditions should remain distinct.)
- [[ray-charles|Ray Charles]] - R&B recordings (Charles brought gospel phrasing into rhythm and blues recordings.)
- [[rock-and-roll|Rock and roll]] - R&B to rock and roll (Early rock and roll drew on the rhythmic language of R&B, alongside country and other sources.)
- [[soul-music|Soul music]] - R&B to soul (Soul grew from R&B while developing a more pronounced gospel-derived vocal language.)
- [[swing-music|Swing music]] - swing roots (Swing horn riffs and small-combo arrangements fed early R&B.)

## Curator Notes

R&B is the junction of documented blues, gospel and swing influences and later soul/rock developments. Do not collapse it into any one source.
