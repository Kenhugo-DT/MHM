# Map Zones

These are the current high-level map zones. They are layout hints, not hard
truths. Bridge nodes can belong to one primary zone and several secondary zones.

## classical-history

Baroque, Classical and Romantic composition. Links to folk and progressive rock
need a named work, practice or artist rather than a vague "classical influence".

## roots-blues

Roots, blues, gospel, rhythm and blues, early electric guitar history and rock
and roll foundations. Gospel is a distinct sacred tradition; connections to
R&B, soul and rock describe documented influence, not identical genres.

## jazz

Early jazz, swing, bebop, cool and modal jazz, jazz guitar and bridges into
fusion, funk and rock. Miles Davis connects several phases without making
those phases interchangeable.

## pop-soul-disco

Soul, Motown, disco and later pop. Gospel and R&B can pull supported artists
toward roots-blues, while the pop zone remains a distinct home.

## folk-country-vise

Folk, acoustic singer-songwriters and Norwegian vise.

## country-roots

Early country, bluegrass, honky-tonk, western swing, Bakersfield and outlaw
country. Western swing reaches into jazz and blues; Bakersfield borrows from
honky-tonk and rockabilly. Outlaw country is partly about recording autonomy,
not one fixed sound.

## rock-circuit

The central rock web: classic rock, glam, major guitar bands and bridge acts.

## psychedelia-prog

Psychedelia, art rock, progressive rock, space rock and progressive metal.

## hard-rock-metal

Hard rock, heavy metal, doom, thrash, power metal and metal-adjacent scenes.

## punk-alt

Punk, post-punk, hardcore, alternative rock and adjacent underground scenes.

## hip-hop-rap

Hip-hop, rap, regional styles and documented crossings into rock and metal.
Keep the scene legible instead of placing all crossovers in a rock zone.

## guitar-workshop

Guitar models, makers, brands and instrument-history connections.
